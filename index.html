Brazil Averaged Six Outpatient Visits per Day for Conditions Associated With Gambling Addiction in 2025

By Alexa Meirelles | July 29, 2026

The 2026 World Cup has barely ended, and the tournament already leaves plenty to talk about. Between political undercurrents, controversial decisions, and a competition played under heavy international tension, it became a topic of conversation far beyond the pitch. In Brazil, what also grabbed attention was the role betting companies played throughout the entire competition and in the broadcast of the matches.

Several studies suggest that betting has become woven into everyday life in Brazil, the fifth largest betting market in the world, according to the international consultancy Regulus Partners and reported by BBC News.

Accounts of how gambling addiction can devastate people's health are not hard to find. Data from the Ministry of Health's Outpatient Information System (Sistema de Informações Ambulatoriais, SIA) show that the country recorded 7,443 outpatient visits between 2015 and 2025 in which the primary or associated diagnosis code was F63.0 — pathological gambling, also known as gambling disorder, an uncontrollable addiction to gambling and betting — or Z72.6, which refers to gambling and betting habits.

The number of cases has risen sharply in recent years. A decade ago, in 2015, just 100 cases were logged across the country. Between 2015 and 2020, the annual total never exceeded 200. Since then, the figure has climbed steadily, reaching 2,209 cases in 2025 through November alone.

Hospitalizations, on the other hand, remain much lower. Still, the number was about 2.4 times higher last year than in 2019, the earliest year with records for these ICD codes. Data from Brazil's Hospital Information System (Sistema de Informações Hospitalares, SIH) show 59 hospitalizations in 2019, rising to a peak of 266 in 2024 before falling to 143 in 2025.

Brazil recorded more than 22 times as many outpatient visits for gambling-related health issues in 2025 as it did a decade earlier

The sports betting market was legalized in 2018, under the administration of former President Michel Temer (MDB), and rules for the sector were established in 2023 through Law No. 14,790/2023, under the current administration of President Lula (PT). It governs the fixed-odds betting market in Brazil, covering online games and virtual online casino games, while also setting rules for companies, taxation, and bettor protection.

The market generated gross revenue of R$ 37 billion in 2025 alone, according to the Ministry of Finance's Secretariat of Prizes and Bets (Secretaria de Prêmios e Apostas do Ministério da Fazenda). Critics of the law, however, point to the social, financial, and health effects caused by the sector's rapid growth.

Between 2015 and 2025, Brazil recorded 7,443 outpatient visits for problems related to gambling addiction.

Rondônia recorded 1,166 outpatient visits related to gambling between 2015 and 2025, the second-highest total in the country.


This stands out because the state has a population of only about 1.6 million people, far smaller than that of São Paulo.

The state of São Paulo alone, which has the country's largest population (46 million), accounts for nearly 44% of all consultations, totalizing 3,273 cases in the 2015-2025 period.

The state capital accounted for the vast majority of cases, with a total of almost 72% of the state and 31% of the country. Nearly all of them took place in the Jardim Paulista neighborhood – more specifically, at the Hospital das Clínicas of the University of São Paulo Faculty of Medicine, according to the healthcare facility records.

The numbers are underreported

Authorities acknowledge that these figures are underreported and that relatively few people seek in-person care. At the beginning of the year, Brazil's public health system (Sistema Único de Saúde, SUS) began offering remote mental health care for people experiencing problems related to gambling and betting, particularly online gambling. The service is free and can be accessed through the "Meu SUS Digital" app.

In December last year, Brazil's Ministry of Finance launched the Centralized Self-Exclusion Platform, which allows people to voluntarily block their access to authorized betting sites. The tool simultaneously blocks all active accounts on licensed betting platforms, prevents users from opening new accounts using the same taxpayer identification number (CPF), and stops betting companies from sending targeted advertising. The latest assessment, released in June, showed that nearly 700,000 people had used the tool.

Warning: Gambling can lead to serious harms to health, including financial stress, relationship breakdown, family violence, mental illness and suicide. If you or someone you know is struggling with gambling, help is available. If you are experiencing suicidal thoughts, contact CVV (Centro de Valorização da Vida) at 188, available 24/7 by phone, or visit cvv.org.br for chat and email support.
